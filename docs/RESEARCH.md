# Student research and alpha protocol

## Interviews before expansion

Recruit 15–25 CS students spanning years, internship experience, certainty, and changed directions. Start with behavior; do not pitch Pathora first.

Ask what CS direction they are considering, how they chose it, what they are learning this week, why they prioritized it, how they judge employer requirements, where they seek advice, when advice conflicts, how they track learning, what makes a project or certification worth doing, and what makes them feel behind. Ask for a recent example instead of a hypothetical preference.

Record: participant code (avoid unnecessary identifying details), situation, observed problem, recent example, current workaround, frequency, consequences, and evidence that contradicts your hypothesis. Obtain consent for any recording; do not upload interviews automatically.

## Dogfooding

Use Pathora for your own exploration and planning. When you leave for notes, spreadsheets, bookmarks, or another task system, log the date, goal, what failed, the workaround, and the underlying need. Do not turn every workaround into a feature. Look for recurrence across other students.

## Alpha: 5–10 students

Without explaining screens, ask participants to sign up, resume onboarding, save two options, compare them, start an experiment, record a reflection, choose a Path, explain the Next Move, add evidence, explain the percentage calculation, and change Paths. Observe failures, hesitation, and misconceptions. Ask whether the result changes what they will work on next.

Record task outcome, point of confusion, severity, and proposed smallest correction. Fix the largest blockers and test again before expanding.

## Closed beta and launch gates

After alpha improvement, invite 25–50 students from varied universities and years. Ask active and inactive students what changed in their work and why they did or did not return. Review meaningful action completion and retention without invented target benchmarks.

Public launch requires reviewed career data, dependable authentication and onboarding, reset/verification/deletion, protected accounts, understandable evidence/progress, mobile/accessibility checks, error/loading/empty states, privacy terms, backups, and a tested hosted deployment. These are outstanding gates, not completed claims.

At 30 days inspect abandoned experiments, stalled milestones, repeated next moves, evidence quality, confusion, and returns. At 90 days decide whether to deepen, revise, or remove features based on behavior and interviews. Expand beyond CS only when the core loop demonstrates value.
